# en-US

The question and the answer, as written in the language.

## Question

You are a native speaker of American English, and you know smartphone apps and how people find apps on Google Play well.

We are writing the title for the English (US) Google Play page of "Caret Launcher", an Android launcher (home screen app). The app's description is in these two files. Read only these two:
- /Users/malt03/Documents/projects/TextLauncher/app/src/main/play/listings/en-US/short-description.txt
- /Users/malt03/Documents/projects/TextLauncher/app/src/main/play/listings/en-US/full-description.txt

The title will be "Caret Launcher - " followed by a subtitle.

Rules for the subtitle:
- At most 13 characters, spaces included. The whole title can be at most 30 characters, and "Caret Launcher - " takes 17.
- Include the word that English-speaking users who know their phones well enough to change their launcher most often use for this kind of app, and would likely search for.
- "Caret Launcher" already contains "Launcher". If that is also the most common word in your language, you do not need to repeat it. In that case, use the next most common word, or words that tell what the app does.
- The Japanese subtitle is 「文字を打って開くランチャー」 ("a launcher you open apps with by typing"). A subtitle close to that meaning is best if it fits the rules, but do not force a translation. Write the best subtitle for English.
- Follow Google Play's rules: no bare keyword lists, no promotional or ranking words such as "best", "#1" or "free", no emoji, and no all caps.

Answer in this form:
- Subtitle: (the subtitle and its number of characters)
- Full title: Caret Launcher - … (number of characters)
- Word included: (the word, and why you think it is the most used and most searched)
- Literal translation into Japanese:
- Other candidates: (1 or 2, each with its number of characters and why you did not choose it)

Do not read any other file, do not use any other tool, and do not write any file.

## Answer

- Subtitle: Type to Open (12 characters)
- Full title: Caret Launcher - Type to Open (29 characters)
- Word included: "Launcher", which is already in the app name, so the subtitle doesn't repeat it. People who change their launcher use "launcher" far more than any other word for this kind of app. The best-known apps carry it in their names (Nova Launcher, Niagara Launcher, Microsoft Launcher, Smart Launcher), and "launcher" is the usual query on Play, alone or with a word in front ("minimal launcher", "keyboard launcher", "search launcher"). The next most common term is "home screen", but casual users say that more than enthusiasts do, and the full description already says "home screen app" in its first line. So the 13 characters go to words that tell what the app does instead. "Type to Open" matches the main claim of the short description, "Type to open apps." It's also the natural way an American user would describe it. "Type to ..." is a familiar pattern ("type to search").
- Literal translation into Japanese: 「入力して開く」 (or 「打って開く」)
- Other candidates:
  - Home Screen (11 characters; full title 28): This is the second most common word, so it would bring a few more searches. But every launcher matches it, and "Caret Launcher - Home Screen" mostly says the same thing twice. It says nothing about typing, which is what makes the app different, and it moves furthest from the Japanese meaning.
  - Keyboard Home (13 characters; full title 30): "Keyboard" is a real search term ("keyboard launcher"), and the description mentions the always-up keyboard. But the phrase sounds odd in English. It could also make people think this is a keyboard (input method) app or a keyboard settings screen, which would hurt installs from people who do find it.
